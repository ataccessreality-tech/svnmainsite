A Plot Site Visit Checklist That Goes Beyond the Entrance

Identify the exact plot, examine access and services, and leave with an evidence-based follow-up list.

Site visits · 2 min read · 11 October 2026

In this article
  1. Prepare before arriving
  2. Find the actual parcel
  3. Follow the services, not just the display
  4. Change the time and conditions
  5. Leave with a short follow-up list

Prepare before arriving

Ask for the approved plan, plot number, survey references and available dimensions before your visit. Confirm the entrance and meeting point. List the questions that matter to your use of the property so the visit is more than a tour of the most attractive features.

Bring a way to take dated notes and photographs, observing privacy and access permissions. Do not enter unsafe or restricted areas. A sales representative can show the site; unresolved boundaries or technical conditions need the right independent professional.

Find the actual parcel

Locate the plot on the plan and at the site. Compare its position, adjoining plots, road and marked boundaries with the documents. Ask how the demarcation was established and whether a survey can confirm an unclear measurement.

Observe the land level, slope and visible occupation. If you see fencing, structures or use that is not explained by the documents, ask for a written response. Do not assume that a numbered marker settles ownership or possession.

Follow the services, not just the display

Ask where water comes from, how electricity connections will be arranged and where drains lead. Record whether each service is available, installed but not commissioned, under construction or proposed. These stages can have very different implications for use.

Check the route from the main road and ask who is responsible for its upkeep. If common facilities are included, ask where they are, who can use them and which fees or rules apply. Keep separately operated facilities distinct from internal layout amenities.

Change the time and conditions

A second visit can expose traffic, noise, lighting or access conditions hidden during a scheduled daytime tour. Where seasonal drainage matters, seek appropriate observations and technical advice. Avoid interpreting a dry site on one day as proof of all-weather performance.

Speak respectfully with neighbours where appropriate and compare observations with official or professional evidence. Informal accounts can identify questions worth asking, but they are not certificates of compliance or ownership.

Leave with a short follow-up list

For each unresolved issue, note what evidence is needed, who should provide it and when you will review it. Keep photographs beside their location and date. Separate what you personally observed from what someone told you.

Use the location handbook to compare visits consistently. If the legal access, plot identity or service arrangements remain unclear, pause the decision until the right documents or assessments answer them.
