Property Budgeting: Look Beyond the Advertised Price

Build a realistic cost worksheet with transaction charges, services, financing and future works.

Budget · 2 min read · 11 October 2026

In this article
  1. Compare the same price basis
  2. Create four cost groups
  3. Check when the money is needed
  4. Price uncertainty openly
  5. Decide on the whole commitment

Compare the same price basis

Before comparing quotations, check the area unit, area measurement and included items. One square yard equals nine square feet, but the conversion only helps if both quotations use comparable areas. Confirm whether the quoted rate applies to the area described in the transaction documents.

Request a total for the exact property. A headline rate may leave out charges or refer to a different plot, size or payment arrangement. Write down the quotation date and validity so an old offer does not distort your shortlist.

Create four cost groups

Group costs into acquisition, finance, making the property usable, and ongoing ownership. Acquisition can include applicable duties, registration charges and professional checks. Finance may include lender charges and interest. Usability costs may involve services, repairs, site preparation or construction.

Ongoing costs can include maintenance, security, taxes and service bills where applicable. Ask who charges each amount, when it becomes due and what it covers. Verify current statutory charges for your transaction rather than borrowing a rate from an old article.

Check when the money is needed

A total can look affordable while the payment timetable is difficult. Put each obligation beside a date and funding source. Allow for a gap between loan approval and actual disbursement, and clarify any conditions affecting the lender release.

Consider whether a delay in selling another asset, obtaining a connection or beginning construction would change your position. Choose a reserve suited to your commitments; a generic emergency-fund figure cannot account for every household.

Price uncertainty openly

Mark estimates as estimates. If service-connection costs, maintenance or construction quantities are not confirmed, obtain more information instead of treating a blank cell as zero. A contingency can help planning, but it should not hide an unresolved material cost.

For a plot, get a design-based construction estimate that matches local development controls. For a built property, arrange appropriate inspection and price foreseeable repairs. The cheapest purchase can become expensive if these costs are overlooked.

Decide on the whole commitment

Compare the total cost with the location, permitted use and legal review. Avoid using a hoped-for resale price or promise of future returns as the only reason the numbers work. Ask how you would manage if resale took longer or the property generated no income.

Use the budget worksheet in the three-check handbook to prepare your questions. Take transaction-specific financing and tax questions to the appropriate adviser before signing.
