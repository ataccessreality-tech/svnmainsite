Three Checks to Make Before Buying Property

A practical starting point: who can sell it, what you can do with it, and whether it works for your life and budget.

Buying basics · 3 min read · 11 October 2026

In this article

  3. 2. Confirm approvals and permitted use

Start with the decision you are actually making

A property purchase often begins with a photograph, a price or a location pin. Turn that first impression into three separate questions: who has the right to sell this property, what use and development are permitted, and whether it suits your purpose at a cost you can manage. These checks apply to the exact parcel or unit, rather than a project name in general.

Write down whether you plan to live there, build later or hold it for another purpose. Your intended use changes the questions you need answered. A weekend destination, for example, may involve very different access and service needs from a primary home.

1. Establish ownership and authority

Request the current deed and the earlier documents that explain the ownership chain. Compare names, survey references, plot details and boundaries. Ask who will sign and receive payment, especially where there are co-owners, inherited interests, a company or a power of attorney.

Use an independent property lawyer to decide which searches and supporting documents are required. An encumbrance certificate and revenue record can help the investigation, but neither is a complete answer on its own. If a discrepancy appears, record it and ask what evidence will resolve it.

2. Confirm approvals and permitted use

Compare the approved layout or building plan with the advertised property. Check the issuing authority, reference, date, survey numbers and conditions. Where RERA registration applies, compare official disclosures with the promoter, location and phase being offered.

Do not assume that a registered sale, residential-looking brochure or marked plot gives permission for any building. Confirm the land use and the permissions your intended construction needs. Keep promised future approvals separate from documents that have already been issued.

3. Test practical fit and total cost

Visit the exact site and travel the route you would actually use. Check access, water, drainage, electricity, neighbouring activity and maintenance arrangements. Put existing facilities and proposed works in different columns.

Request an itemized quotation and allow for applicable transaction charges, professional checks, finance costs and services or construction still to be arranged. The right shortlist is one whose legal, practical and budget questions can all be answered.

Before the first payment

Keep a one-page decision record: documents received, unresolved issues, responsible person and response deadline. Obtain the draft terms and understand cancellation and refund conditions before paying. If someone pressures you to pay while basic information is missing, pause and resolve the gaps.

Use the free handbook to prepare the conversation. It is a general organizing tool; the final review must fit the property and your circumstances.

General buyer education. This article does not assess any specific property or replace transaction-specific legal, tax, financial or technical advice.
