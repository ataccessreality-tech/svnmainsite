Before you buy

What are the three checks I should make before buying property?

Check ownership and the seller's authority, confirm the approvals and permitted use for the exact property, and test the location and total cost against your needs. Keep unresolved points in writing before paying. The three-check handbook includes a printable worksheet.

What should a first-time property buyer do first?

Write down your purpose, budget ceiling, purchase timeline and non-negotiable needs. Decide whether you want a ready home, a plot for building or land for another permitted purpose before comparing advertisements.

What is the difference between land and a residential plot?

Land is a broad term for an area of ground. A residential plot is a defined parcel intended for residential use, usually within a layout. Its name or boundary stones alone do not establish ownership, planning approval or permission to build.

Is buying a plot the same as buying a house?

No. A plot purchase usually leaves design, building permissions, construction, services and future maintenance to be arranged. A house purchase adds checks on the building, condition, sanctioned plans and applicable completion or occupancy documents.

How do I compare two properties fairly?

Use the same area unit and include all acquisition costs. Compare permitted use, legal access, approvals, usable dimensions, services, travel times and written delivery commitments. A lower advertised rate can conceal a higher overall cost.

Does a low price mean a good property deal?

A low price is a reason to investigate, not proof of value. Ask whether access, title, services, restrictions, pending works or resale demand explain the difference. Avoid letting a discount replace your checks.

Should I rely on a broker's verbal assurance?

Ask for documents and written terms from the authorized seller. A broker can help with introductions and site visits, but claims about ownership, approvals, future roads or returns need independent verification.

Can I buy property without visiting it?

It increases the chance of missing access, boundary, drainage and neighbourhood issues. Arrange an independent visit or inspection if you cannot attend, and reconcile the physical property with its documents before committing.

What information should I keep in my property comparison folder?

Keep the location pin, survey and plot details, dated quotation, documents received, inspection notes, photographs, questions outstanding and written replies. Record who supplied each item and when.

Ownership and land documents

What is a sale deed?

A sale deed records a transfer of property between the parties. Ask a property lawyer to examine its execution, registration where required, property description, seller's rights and earlier documents. Possessing a copy alone is not a complete title investigation.

What are link documents or the chain of title?

They are earlier deeds and other records that explain how ownership passed to the current seller. The required investigation depends on the property's history; let your lawyer identify the period and missing links rather than using a universal checklist period.

What is an encumbrance certificate, or EC?

An EC reports registered transactions found for the property and search period. It can help identify recorded transfers or mortgages. Match the property description and obtain clarification about missing or inconsistent entries through the registration office and your lawyer.

Does a nil EC prove that land is dispute-free?

No. A nil result does not rule out every unregistered claim, dispute, search error or issue outside the period searched. Read it alongside the title chain, seller authority, possession and other relevant checks.

Do mutation or revenue records prove ownership?

Mutation and revenue entries serve revenue administration purposes and do not by themselves confer title. Compare them with the deeds and other evidence. Have unexplained differences resolved before relying on the seller's claim.

What are Adangal, 1-B and survey records used for?

In Andhra Pradesh, relevant revenue and survey records can help identify the parcel, recorded interests, classification and boundaries. Their purpose differs from a sale deed. Ask the local revenue or survey office and your lawyer which current records apply.

How do I verify the seller's authority to sell?

Match identity with ownership documents and check co-owners, inheritance, company authorization or a power of attorney where relevant. A lawyer should examine the authority and any limits; a person showing the site may not have authority to sign.

What if a seller is using a power of attorney?

Ask your lawyer to check the instrument, execution and authentication requirements, scope, continuing validity and whether it authorizes this transaction. A power of attorney is an authority document and is not itself a sale deed transferring ownership.

What should I do when plot area or boundaries do not match?

Pause the transaction and request a survey and written reconciliation of the deed, approved plan and on-ground measurements. Do not assume a verbal promise to correct the record will resolve the discrepancy.

Approvals and permitted use

What is RERA registration?

It is registration with the applicable real estate regulator under the RERA framework where required. Review the official project disclosures and current status. It is separate from planning approval and does not replace an independent title review.

How do I check a project's RERA details in Andhra Pradesh?

Use the official AP RERA portal. Compare the name, promoter, location, phase and registration number with your documents, and review available disclosures and updates. Save a dated copy of what you checked.

Is every property required to have a RERA registration?

Applicability and exemptions depend on the Act, state rules and the nature and stage of the project. Do not infer an exemption from a seller's statement alone. Ask for the written basis and get professional clarification if registration is absent.

Is layout approval the same as RERA registration?

No. Layout approval concerns the approved development plan; RERA registration concerns the applicable regulatory framework and disclosures. Check both where relevant, including whether your exact plot and phase are covered.

What should I check on an approved layout plan?

Check the issuing authority, approval reference, date, survey numbers, plot number, dimensions, roads, open spaces, reservations and conditions. Compare the approved version with the sale plan and physical site.

Can I build a house immediately after buying a plot?

Not automatically. Confirm residential use, applicable building permission, setbacks, road access, service connections and local restrictions. Layout approval does not necessarily give individual permission for your proposed building.

Can agricultural land always be used for a home?

No. Ownership, land classification, permitted use and current conversion or planning requirements must be checked. In Andhra Pradesh, ask the competent authority about the current procedure for the parcel; do not assume purchase or registration changes its use.

What is land-use zoning?

Zoning describes the uses and development controls assigned to an area in the applicable planning framework. Check the current official plan and conditions for the exact parcel, especially if you intend a commercial activity or a particular building type.

What restrictions can affect coastal or waterfront property?

Applicable coastal, waterbody, environmental, flood-risk and planning restrictions may affect use or construction. Ask the relevant authority and a qualified professional to check the exact location. Proximity to the coast does not establish buildability.

Choosing a location

How do I choose the best location for a property?

Start with your intended use and daily routine. Compare legal access, measured travel times, services, neighbourhood conditions, environmental risks and affordability. A location that suits a holiday visit may not suit everyday living.

How should I measure distance to work, schools or hospitals?

Measure the usable route from the exact site entrance, not a straight line from a broad map pin. Visit at relevant times and record typical travel time, road condition and alternative routes.

Should I buy near a proposed airport or highway?

Treat proposed infrastructure as uncertain until you check official announcements and status. Compare the property's present usefulness and cost first. A project announcement does not guarantee completion dates, access or price growth.

How do I check whether a property has legal road access?

Ask for the documents or approved plan establishing access and have them reviewed. A visible track across someone else's land or an informal arrangement may not provide the right of access you need.

What should I ask about water availability?

Ask about source, connection arrangements, reliability, seasonal availability, testing, treatment and recurring cost. A tank or pipeline seen during a visit does not establish year-round supply to your property.

Why should I check drainage before buying?

Poor drainage can affect access and usability during rain. Look at site levels, drains, outlets and nearby low areas; request technical clarification where needed. Dry-weather photographs are not a substitute for a drainage assessment.

How do I check flood or coastal risk?

Review available official hazard information, local history and site levels, and seek technical advice for the parcel. Neighbours' observations can help identify questions but do not replace an appropriate assessment.

Is a gated layout always the best location?

Gates may influence access management, but suitability still depends on approvals, services, connectivity, maintenance obligations and your needs. Compare written responsibilities and recurring costs rather than choosing on the entrance design alone.

How can I compare neighbourhoods without guessing future growth?

Record what exists now: occupied homes, transport, shops, services and employment access. Keep proposed facilities in a separate column with an official source and status. Avoid treating advertised future growth as a certainty.

Budget and total cost

What costs should I include beyond the property price?

Allow for applicable duties and registration charges, professional checks, financing charges, taxes where applicable, infrastructure or maintenance charges and any construction or service-connection costs. Request an itemized quotation and verify current rates for your transaction.

How do I compare price per square yard and square foot?

One square yard equals nine square feet. Divide the square-yard rate by nine for the equivalent square-foot rate, then check the same area basis and included charges. Do not compare a gross-area quote with a different usable-area quote.

Is government market value the same as the selling price?

No. A value used for registration purposes and a negotiated market price serve different purposes. Ask the registration office or your adviser about the applicable valuation and charges for the specific transaction.

What should a written quotation include?

It should identify the property, measured area, price basis, all charges, payment schedule, validity, inclusions and exclusions. Ask who receives each payment and how taxes or other variable amounts will be calculated.

Does bank loan approval mean the property is legally safe?

A lender's assessment is for its own lending decision. It does not replace your independent title review, inspection or contract advice. Ask what documents and conditions the lender relied on.

How much emergency money should I retain after buying?

Choose a buffer based on your income stability, existing commitments and likely expenses, with advice where needed. Avoid committing all available cash without allowing for unexpected transaction, repair or service costs.

Should I pay a token amount to reserve a property?

Understand the purpose, recipient, refund conditions and time limits in writing first. Complete the checks appropriate to the commitment and get advice on applicable agreement and advance-payment rules before paying.

Can a seller promise price growth or rental income?

Treat such claims cautiously. Ask for the contractual basis, counterparty obligations, costs and conditions, and seek independent advice. Future prices, occupancy and resale timing are uncertain.

How do I plan a construction budget after buying a plot?

Obtain a design-based estimate covering approvals, site preparation, structure, finishes, utilities, professional fees and contingency. Match the estimate to local development controls and your funding timeline before relying on a generic cost per square foot.

Site visits and physical checks

What should I carry on a property site visit?

Carry the approved plan if available, plot details, a map, your question list and a phone for dated notes and photos. Arrange access safely and use a surveyor for reliable boundary or area verification.

How do I identify the exact plot at the site?

Match its number, survey reference, dimensions and position on the approved plan with the physical site. Ask for an authorized demarcation and professional verification if the boundary is unclear.

What should I photograph during a visit?

Record access roads, plot markers, adjacent properties, levels, drains and visible services, with permission where required. Add the date and location to your notes; photographs support observation but do not certify ownership or compliance.

Should I visit a site more than once?

A second visit at another time can reveal traffic, noise, lighting and service conditions that a single scheduled visit misses. If rain or seasonal issues matter, obtain suitable observations or technical checks.

How do I check electricity readiness?

Ask whether infrastructure is installed, energized and available for individual connection, and request the responsible utility's requirements and charges. Poles or a transformer alone do not establish a usable connection for your plot.

What questions should I ask about maintenance?

Ask who maintains roads, lighting, security and common facilities, what charges apply, when they begin and how responsibilities transfer. Request the rules and terms in writing.

Are artistic renders proof of completed amenities?

No. Ask which features are completed, under construction or proposed, with dates, location and written scope. Compare physical progress with the relevant approved plan and contract commitments.

When do I need a surveyor or engineer?

Use a surveyor for unresolved boundaries or measurements and an engineer for site, drainage, soil or building concerns. Their roles differ from a lawyer's title and transaction review.

What are useful warning signs during a site visit?

Unclear plot identity, blocked access, inconsistent dimensions, visible waterlogging, unexplained occupation or pressure to pay before receiving documents all deserve clarification. Record the issue and pause until it is adequately resolved.

Agreements, registration and resources

What is the difference between an agreement for sale and a sale deed?

An agreement sets the proposed transaction's terms and obligations; a sale deed records the transfer. Their legal effect and registration requirements need professional review for your transaction. Do not treat a booking receipt as a completed transfer.

What should I check before signing a property agreement?

Check the parties, property description, area, price, payment dates, obligations, delivery terms, cancellation, refund, defaults and dispute provisions. Ensure important promises are included and have the draft reviewed before signing.

What should cancellation and refund terms explain?

They should state when cancellation is allowed, deductions if any, process, documents required and repayment timelines. Ask how disputed amounts are handled and whether the terms comply with applicable law.

How should I make and record property payments?

Use an authorized, traceable channel to the verified contractual recipient. Keep receipts, references, dates and the purpose of each payment. Investigate requests to pay unrelated accounts or undocumented amounts before proceeding.

What should I verify just before registration?

Reconfirm the parties and authority, final deed, property details, payment reconciliation, relevant current records and any release or consent needed. Ask your lawyer and the registration office about the transaction-specific requirements.

What documents should I retain after registration?

Keep the registered deed and available certified copies, payment records, possession or handover record, approvals, survey information and relevant correspondence. Ask about subsequent revenue, tax or utility updates that apply.

Does possession mean I own the property?

Physical possession and legal title are different questions. Check the transfer documents, registration requirements and circumstances with your lawyer. A handover photograph or possession letter alone does not answer every ownership question.

Are the downloadable buyer handbooks free?

Yes. The resource library offers free PDFs without registration or a lead form. They provide general buyer education and printable checklists, rather than project brochures.

Where can I read more or get help with a specific property?

Read the buyer blog and download the handbooks. For a transaction, use an independent property lawyer, surveyor or other qualified adviser as appropriate. For SVN project documents, use the contact page.
